Quick Guide

Click here to open detailed annotation protocol pop-up
🔴 Negative Stance

if the reply post encourages readers to at least partially doubt, or dismiss the study's main conclusion.

Common patterns (not exhaustive):

  • A1: Post criticizes the study’s data, methodology, or interpretations
  • A2: Post criticizes the study’s authors, funding, or source
  • A3: Post provides counter-evidence or calls the study outdated
  • A4: Post questions the study’s relevance or applicability
  • A5: Post uses sarcasm or rhetorical questions against the conclusion
  • A6: Post dismisses the study without engaging with its content
  • A7: Post casts generic doubt without a specific complaint

Note: Check if a pattern actually functions to lower trust in the conclusion. A post that raises one of these points and then explicitly rejects or outweighs it (e.g., "it's old, but still holds up") is NOT negative.

🟢 Non-negative Stance

if the reply post expresses a stance toward the main conclusion that is not negative, i.e., it endorses, accepts, reports, or reacts positively to it.

Common patterns (not exhaustive):

  • Z1: Post reports parts of the study or summarizes it (e.g., displays the paper's abstract)
  • Z2: Post agrees with, endorses, or reacts positively to the study
  • Z3: Post defends or supports the conclusion indirectly (e.g., by criticizing those who deny or attack it)
  • Z4: Post accepts the conclusion as given (e.g., builds on it, presupposes it)
⚪ No Stance

if the reply post expresses no stance toward the main conclusion at all.

Common patterns (not exhaustive):
  • N1: Unclear connection to the paper (e.g., "Why doesn't the media report about it?")
  • N2: Posts directed at other users or the thread (e.g., "@userABC, why do you think that?")
  • N3: Mere mention or link provision (e.g., "This paper was already shared yesterday")
  • N4: Honest information-seeking questions (e.g., "What did they use for identifying the critical threshold?")

Note: Only for posts that genuinely take no position on the conclusion, NOT for posts you find hard to read.

Step-by-step Decision

To arrive at consistent decisions, always follow these steps:

1. Does this post encourage readers to at least partially doubt, or dismiss the study's main conclusion? -‍> 🔴 Negative Stance

Notes:

  • posts may combine praise and criticism so that patterns from Negative Stance and Non-negative Stance are met. Negative Stance takes precedence if it is not rejected or outweighed within the post itself.
  • check that the Negative Stance is aimed at the study and not just at deniers, the sharer, corporations, politicians, or other commenters

2. If not, does the post express a stance toward the main conclusion that is not negative, i.e., it endorses, accepts, reports, or reacts positively to it? -‍> 🟢 Non-negative Stance

4. Otherwise, if the post expresses no stance toward the main conclusion at all -‍> ⚪ No Stance
