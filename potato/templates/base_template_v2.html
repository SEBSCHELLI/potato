Annotation Protocol

A Negative Citation occurs when a social media post encourages readers to partially or fully question, doubt, or dismiss the conclusions of the referenced study.

Criteria for Negative Citations:

Post criticizes the study’s data, methodology, or interpretations, etc., and/or proposes better study design choices

Examples:

  • Flaws in the study’s design (sample too small, wrong method, claims too strong)
  • Proposes a more realistic study design instead (better method, different sample)

Post criticizes the study’s authors or source

Examples:

  • Conflicts of interest, author’s questionable track of record
  • Not peer-reviewed, questionable journal / venue

Post provides counter-evidence and/or implies the existence of more recent evidence

Examples:

  • Counter-evidence (studies with contrary findings, anecdotal counter-evidence)
  • Calling the study outdated or old, implying more recent studies exist, which could be both in line or contrary to the study’s conclusions

Post questions the study’s relevance or applicability

Examples:

  • Findings in basic research might not apply to real-world scenarios or scenarios readers care about
